1Atoms

The smallest pieces. On their own they don't say much; every larger piece is built from these.

Neutrals

Pure white paper, chrome, three inks and two rules. No accent color: the captured colors are the only color.

Type

One family, SF Pro, in two voices: big names at a regular weight with tight tracking, and small technical captions with tabular figures. Hierarchy comes from size and ink, never bold, so it reads less like a website or a tool and more like a physical label. Only the native shell keeps macOS's own weights.

Swatch

Square in the archive, where color is studied. A faint inset edge lets light colors read on white paper.

2Molecules

Atoms joined into small pieces that do one job: say what a color is called, where it came from, or what it's close to.

Name block

Your name first and big, in full ink. Until you name it, the placeholder sits in gray. The name is the handle and the meaning; the hex underneath keeps the exact color.

Source

Where the color came from and when: the kind of source, the place, and its title.

Neighbors

The 8 samples around the picked pixel, with the color in the middle. Color only makes sense in context.

Similar

The colors you've already kept that are close to this one, each with where it came from and when. No count and no score: your own history, not a measure. Where Sip deletes repeats, Palit lets you find them.

Palette

The colors you kept together in one ⇧-click run, side by side on 1 px ink rules. Each stays its own capture. In a color's story the strip marks that color and opens the others, and the palette can be named there any time.

Draft copy: the placeholder, and “4 colors, No. 029–032”

Color chip

A color, its name and its number on one line. The menu bar popover lists your latest colors this way.

Name beside the cursor

3Organisms

Complete parts of the interface, built from molecules. Each one can stand on its own in a screen.

Desktop palette

Your latest picks, or a palette you pin, on the desktop. Docked at the edge as thin bars that open when you point at them. Drag it off the edge to sit anywhere, and it snaps back at the edge. Click copies the hex, ⌥-click RGB, ⌘-click OKLCH. A new pick lands in it with a small ring, so there's no chip.

Draft copy: “Copied …”, “Pinned”, “Unpin”

Point at it, click a color to copy, or drag it by its top.

Archive tile

A square of color, the name or placeholder, the number and the place. Tiles sit on a grid of 1 px ink lines.

Replaced in the archive window by days with their places, with no grid lines (A57, A72); kept here as v1's.

Story panel

Everything Palit kept about one color, in reading order: your name, a note of your own, identity, source, its palette when it has one, neighbors, how it reads, and the similar colors you've kept.

Replaced in the archive window by the specimen card (A60, A61); kept here as v1's.

6The archive window, v3

Prototype v3 (design/v3/) is the final prototype, and now the source of truth for the window's look: v2 merged, with Noah's picks from the v3 workshop and a polish pass. Every example here runs on v3's own CSS and archive, at the window's real width.

The polish

One type scale, one gray, one left edge and one motion, so the window reads as one thing. Nothing sits on the colors, and nothing is cut off: names, places and file names wrap instead of ending in an ellipsis.

The bar

Palit's own bar, not a Mac toolbar: white, with Days, Palettes and Spectrum as words in the middle and the one you're on underlined in ink. On the right, a magnifier opens into a search field, or press ⌘F; then − and + with no dots. The zoom steps aside outside Days.

Copy from v3: “Search”, and each control's hover label

The letters' row

ROYGBIV in a row of its own under the bar, still on top, with the count at the row's end. That end is where everything else shows too: the zoom's stop for a moment, what just happened with its colors, and what you've gathered with ⇧-click. Nothing floats over the colors.

Copy from v3: the messages, and “2 selected”

Days, by their dates

The window opens on today. Each day is labeled by its date and nothing else: Today at 26px, other days at 17px, with no counts and no arrow, and a click on the date still opens the day out. An unnamed color shows on two lines, the color word and then where it's from, dimmed on the color.

Click a date to open that day out. Click a color for its card, ⇧-click to gather, right-click for its menu; − and + step through the zoom.

Zoom, four stops

Still four stops, each its own idea: only color, your names, and where, then the hunt. There are no dots now: − and + step through them, or ⌘− and ⌘+, and the stop's name shows at the row's end for a moment. And where puts the place on one line at 13px and where it lives on the next at 11px in gray; in the hunt, places are 13px too.

Copy from v3: the names of the stops

Spectrum, and the name you can change

Spectrum is a tab again, and the fan is as it was: 168 by 274 cards, 12° apart, the chosen card lifted 124px. Once your colors fill a full circle, 30 or more, it loops: past violet and the neutrals, red comes round again. The chosen color's name at the top left is yours to change in place: click and type, Enter or Esc ends it, and an empty name shows its placeholder, dimmed.

Copy from v3: “Click to rename”

Click a card, or use ← and →, to turn the fan, and click the chosen card to open it. Click the name to rename it.

A palette, as a study

A palette opens as a study: each color large, then on white, mid gray and black, with where it came from and its codes. Try on the table takes the palette's colors to the table, inside Palettes.

Copy from v3: “Copy the codes”, “Pin to the desktop”, “Try on the table”, “Keep this version”, and the line under the study

The table, inside Palettes

There's no Table tab: the table lives inside Palettes. Try on the table from any palette, or right-click any color and Take to table, and the table opens with a Palettes link back at the top left. Drag the colors around, set them on white, mid gray or black, and bring one in by its name.

Copy from v3: “Show names”, “Clear the table”, “Keep as a palette”, “Bring a color here by its name”

Drag a color to move it, double-click to open it, right-click for its menu.

The specimen card

A color opens as the specimen card, over the window. Similar says only where and when, and Also from here stays. The labels sit in a 96px column, the rows are 13px, the trail and the scratch pad are 11px and the name is 50px. Every color beside text is as tall as the text's capitals and sits on its baseline: Similar's squares, and the strips, which run to the card's edge, or halfway when they hold one color; a palette's strip takes the right half of the row. The card fades out the way it came in.

Copy from v3, including “Scratch pad” and “Click the name to give it yours.”

Close it with ×, Esc or a click outside it; click any color behind it to open that one.

Right-click menu

Right-click any color for a Mac menu: Take to table first, then Add to palette, Pin, Copy hex and Share. Right-click what you've gathered with ⇧-click and it acts on all of them, under a line with their colors. Here it's held open on two of today's colors, and its items work.
