Executive Synthesis

Locked Product & Design Architecture

Under delegated executive authorship while Noah is away, here are the finalized design architecture decisions for Palette. These fuse Noah’s creative brief (Pantone chips, collectible analog paper objects, minimal Aesop packaging, and Albers eye-training) into a quiet, native macOS utility. Check the boxes to record your sign-offs (auto-saved to browser).

EXECUTIVE DECISION 1
Micro-Ticket Capture Dispenser

Standard floating notification banners are discarded. Color picks dispense as a perforated physical ticket chip (32×46pt) directly downward from the menu bar iris like an industrial dispenser.

Visual reference: Ravex aerosol cap + perforated concert ticket.
Acoustics: Soft synthesized sine ping (880Hz → 440Hz, 180ms ease-out).
Physics: Slips into the Today row. Hovering expands; clicking opens the card. Never steals user focus.
EXECUTIVE DECISION 2
Industrial Specimen Card (40/60)

The capture card is an analog paper artifact: top 40% is an unvarnished solid block of the stolen color; bottom 60% is a high-density technical typography label separated by a stark 2.5px solid rule.

Visual reference: Aesop × RIMOWA Köln label + Ravex industrial cans.
Stock: Warm neutral cotton paper (#FBFBFA) with 0.5px hairline boundary.
Typography: 34pt bold tabular number, 22pt sentence-case title, 12pt tabular coordinates. No mono fonts (A11).
EXECUTIVE DECISION 3
Dual-Engine Dashboard: Ledger + Radial Fan

The archive window rejects cluttered dashboard widgets in favor of two focused viewing engines: a chronological Specimen Ledger and a tactile Núria Garcia Radial Fan Deck.

Ledger engine: Chronological inventory sorted by date and locality with contextual extraction crops.
Radial Fan engine: Circular spectrum wheel sorted by OKLCH hue angle with spring rotation physics.
Navigation: Library, Curated Swatch Rings (Palettes), and Localities (Safari, Figma, Photos).
EXECUTIVE DECISION 4
Curated Swatch Rings & Albers Laboratory

Palettes are reimagined as physical brass swatch rings rather than dry software folders. An integrated Interaction of Color workbench actively trains the designer's eye without automating taste.

Swatch Ring: Cards bound on a brass ring; drag to reorder and study color rhythm.
Albers Lab: Test simultaneous contrast across White, 50% Gray, Dark Olive, and Pitch Black.
Friction on purpose: Descriptors (warmth, value, chroma, ΔE) explain and train, never prescribe.

1. 40/60 Golden Industrial Ratio

40% of the card height (192pt) is dedicated to pure, unvarnished color. This prevents UI chrome from diluting color perception while reserving 60% (288pt) for packaging-grade typographic specifications.

2. The Aesop × RIMOWA 2.5px Rule

Borrowing directly from the Köln travel kit packaging, a stark 2.5px solid ink rule (#1D1D1F) cleanly separates the chromatic block from the technical white paper, establishing unmistakable tactile authority.

3. No Mono Fonts (Rule A11)

All technical values (HEX, RGB, OKLCH, Display P3) are set in SF Pro with tabular numbers (monospacedDigit()). Numbers align with surgical precision without the sterile programmer feel of monospace typefaces.

4. Simultaneous Contrast Testing

The mini ground switcher on the front face tests Josef Albers' primary theorem: surrounding brightness changes the perceived value and chroma of the central swatch in real time.
