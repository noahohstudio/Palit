## Presentation Structure (Key Takeaway)

* Recommended narrative arc, roughly:
   1. State the problem (~10%): get the audience to agree the problem is real before moving on
   2. Demo the solution (~20-25%): show the thing working end-to-end
   3. Deep dives (~60-70%): 2-3 areas where important design decisions were made
* Deep dives are where you show design thinking depth
   * Pick the 2-3 questions that had the most important design decisions
   * Show 2-3 approaches explored, land on the final one and explain why
   * This is also where you share cool explorations that didn’t make the cut
* Naheel’s preference: start with the solution, then backfill with thinking and tradeoffs
   * Gets the audience bought in before loading them with context

## Deep Dives: Why They Matter

* The mark of seniority: identifying which parts of a problem need the deepest exploration
* Junior instinct is to just show a simple solution; senior instinct is to isolate the key levers
* Deep dives show: “here’s how I think, here’s how I break apart problems”
* Applies beyond competition: every portfolio presentation and interview should have this

## Project 1: Figma Mobile (Live Comments)

* Team explored a mobile-first Figma experience for capturing fleeting design ideas
* Three directions explored: Quick Capture, AI Copilot (Figgy AI), Live Comments
   * Landed on Live Comments: voice + visual annotation, no canvas editing, low friction
* Three navigation deep dives: Figma Feed (AI grouping), Lasso Checkpoints, Snap to Frame
   * Snap to Frame was the strongest: double-tap to lock a frame, works well with annotations
* Key crit feedback:
   * Stuck between two use cases: “get it out of my head” vs. “give feedback to my team”
      * Solving for both risks solving neither
   * Virgil Abloh reference: creative directors giving rapid mobile feedback over WhatsApp was a strong framing angle the team underused
   * Recommendation: stress-test the design through one specific persona (lead, CD, PM, copywriter)
   * Phone may not be the right form factor for all personas (iPad, tablet worth considering)
   * AI summary feature noted as interesting but underexplored for async decision-making
* Team won the competition despite the tough crit
   * Depth of thinking and willingness to explore multiple directions drove the win
   * Crit gave them a clear direction to keep iterating

## Project 2: Threads on TV

* Exploratory brief: can short-form text apps (Threads, Reddit, X) work on TV?
* Three versions iterated:
   * V1: literal Threads-to-TV translation, one thread at a time, auto-scroll, ElevenLabs audio
   * V2: ambient screensaver mode, content grouped under trending topics
   * V3: AI-generated 24-hour personalized channel with an avatar host
      * Avatar persona adapts to user taste (newscaster, streamer, talk show host)
      * Sourced entirely from live text content, faster than YouTube/news coverage
* Key crit feedback (Nate, founding designer at Browser Company, now at Anthropic on Claude):
   * Strong journey from basic text display to personalized news channel
   * Needs a crisper pitch: why would someone want Threads on TV when they have a phone?
   * Personalized channel concept is genuinely interesting, not just a gimmick
   * Open question: how does the user tell the TV what they want to see?
      * Likely different from their phone Threads algorithm

## On Crits and Feedback

* Getting “no notes” is actually a bad sign: means you didn’t engage people or peel back enough layers
* A good crit gives the team exactly what to work on next
* Teams often reach a much stronger place after iterating on crit feedback
* Industry is small: the people in the room (Meta Reality Labs, Google VR, Cognition, Reddit, Anthropic) are the people you’ll cross paths with repeatedly
