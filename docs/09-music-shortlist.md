## How it should sit

Claude's take, not Noah's: quiet drum and bass can work if it's sparse, with no vocals or vocal chops, and sits far enough under the voices that you only notice it when someone pauses. Duck it under every line, let it come up for the statement screens and the cuts between sections, and keep the Wood sound above it. None of these pages says whether a track has a half-time feel, and only one lists a tempo, so that part needs your ears.

## The YouTube Audio Library

It lives in YouTube Studio, which needs your sign-in, so Claude couldn't browse it. In Studio, open the Audio Library, filter the genre to Ambient or Dance & Electronic, and filter the license to "Attribution not required". YouTube's help page says Audio Library downloads won't be claimed through Content ID, and that its Creative Commons tracks still need a credit in the description. None of the help pages Claude found says whether the music can be used off YouTube, so if the video goes anywhere else, check the terms shown in Studio.

## Where the video goes, and the licenses

Pixabay's license lets you use the music for free without credit; it only rules out things like selling the track on its own. Uppbeat's free plan is "for individual use on your own channels", which covers the video on your own channel; for a submission hosted elsewhere, check with Uppbeat. CC BY allows any use, with the credit.

## A recommendation to listen from

Claude's suggestion: start with Modern Drum And Bass, or Immersion if you're happy to make a free Uppbeat account, under the statements and the demo, where the video should feel most alive. Under the deep dives, try nothing at all first, then the realization at a whisper. If drum and bass fights the voices in any section, drop it there; music that's barely noticed is doing its job.
