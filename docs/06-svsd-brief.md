## The brief, word for word

> **Design Brief:**
> **The Search for Top Young Design Talent**
>
> **The Challenge**
> The way we build software has changed.
> In the past, building a profitable software product often meant designing for a large group of people. Today, it's possible to build for much smaller, more specific audiences.
> Pick a specific group of people and design a product they'll love, even if others might hate it.
> There's just one constraint: Your product must center around the theme of “storytelling.”
> Interpret storytelling however you like. It could be a reading experience, a tool for creating presentations, a motion graphics editor, a way to preserve family stories across generations, or something entirely different.
> I’m leaving this intentionally open-ended because I want everyone to come out of this with their own unique portfolio project.
>
> **Criteria**
> 1. Product Thinking
> How you frame the problem, identify a compelling solution, and recognize which deeper problems within your product are worth exploring and solving
> 2. Depth of Thinking & Communication
> How rigorously do you explore your ideas, what are the decisions you make along the way, and how clearly do you communicate your reasoning.
> 3. Craft
> The quality of your execution. Everything from the visual and interaction design of your product to its motion, details, and the presentation itself.
>
> **Your Submission**
> Your final submission should tell the story of your project in a video
> (7 mins max, 10 mins if you believe you have something super interesting to say for that long – but it better be an entertaining 10mins)
> Show us the problem, your solution, and most importantly – how you explored it, and the interesting challenges you identified and solved along the way.
> Beyond that, how you present your work is entirely up to you – could be a video of you presenting a deck, prototype, website, or whatever combination of things
> Use your own judgment and creativity. The way you communicate your work is part of the work.

## Judging Criteria and What Wins

* Holistic, not rubric-based: no strict per-category scoring
* Key question: are the design decisions intentional, or does it feel like a Claude one-shot?
* Ability to articulate and communicate intentionality matters as much as the work itself
* Finalists chosen partly on crit potential: will this spark interesting discussion in New York?
* 3–4 finalist teams (9–12 people) flown to New York

## What "Opinionated Product" Means

* Not about controversy; about designing for a specific user group's unmet needs
* The opinion is the design decision that makes the product hyper-great for one group, even if it alienates others
   * Snapchat example: started on camera, not inbox; messages disappear; deliberately alienates older users
   * Email app example: can't reach inbox zero by design, loved by some, hated by others
* Niche can be large or small, psychographic rather than demographic
   * Example: background music listeners who want a Nest-like panel, not a Spotify discovery feed
* Apple's apps are deliberately unopinionated because grandma and a toddler both use them

## Prototype Expectations

* No App Store submission required; functional demo is fine
* Even an unfunctional prototype is acceptable if the video is compelling
* Sleight-of-hand presentation can make a limited prototype feel real
* Hooking up a real AI API is strongly recommended:
   * You learn what the model is good and bad at
   * Shows you can craft an AI product
* Vibe Coding 101 session at the end of the call to cover this

## Video Submission Structure

* Up to 10 minutes, but keep it to 7 unless it earns the extra time
* Rough split: ~40% product demo, ~60% deep-dive process
* Pick 2–3 meaty design challenges as the "body paragraphs" of the video
   * Show what you tried, what didn't work, and how you got to the final solution
   * Example themes from a past project: privacy perception, memory resurfacing, manual vs. AI capture spectrum
* Show iterations, but curate: pick 2 interesting directions, not all 7
* Link to prototype is fine, but only gets opened if the video earns it

## Deep-Dive Topics: What Makes Them Strong

* Not "here's how we picked our colors" but meaty, cross-industry challenges
   * Retention mechanics, privacy tradeoffs, AI accuracy vs. user effort
* Brother's Facebook Portal project as a model:
   1. How to introduce memory capture without feeling like surveillance
   2. How to resurface memories at the right moment so they don't just sit in a file
   3. Designing the manual-to-AI spectrum for accurate but low-effort capture
* That project led to an internship on the exact team working on the same problem

## Remote Collaboration Advice

* Remote is normal in industry: OpenAI design team split SF/NY, Airbnb fully remote
* Cowork on Discord even without talking: builds accountability and chemistry
* Structure crit sessions in two halves:
   * First half: "yes-and" everything, push every idea as far as it can go
   * Second half: intentionally critical, converge on the strongest direction
* "When it's on your laptop, it's yours. When it's on the wall, it's ours." (72andSunny)

## Project To-Do List

* Saturday–Sunday: Explore multiple approaches to each big rock. Document why you chose or rejected each direction. Build the prototype in small chunks, prioritizing a convincing core journey over full functionality.
* As you work: Record assumptions, alternatives, tests, and reasons for design decisions. State assumptions made because of limited time.

## Working Project Brief Guidelines

1. Audience: Who is this for? A psychographic or workflow-based group is fine; it needn't be tiny.
2. Unmet need: What does this group need that general-purpose products serve poorly?
3. Product opinion: What intentional design decision makes the product better for this group, even if it is less useful to others? Opinionated does not mean controversial for its own sake.
4. Core journey: What will the demo show to make that opinion tangible?
5. Big rocks: Name 2–3 substantive design challenges. For each, list approaches to explore and what will help you choose among them.
6. Prototype boundary: Decide what must work and what can be simulated. If AI is central, consider testing a real integration to understand the model's strengths and limitations.
7. Presentation story: Show both the product and the thinking behind it. The suggested, nonbinding balance is roughly 40% problem/product/demo and 60% process deep dive. Curate a few meaningful iterations rather than every version.

Quality check: Can someone explain who the product is for, what deliberate choice makes it different, and how the exploration led to that choice?
